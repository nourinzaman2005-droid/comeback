# ComeBack: Demo Day presentation script

**Event:** ICC x Ignyte Hackathon final, Start-up Stage, Dubai AI Festival, Dubai World Trade Centre
**When:** Tuesday 27 October 2026, 1pm to 2pm
**Format:** 3 minute pitch, then 2 minutes of questions from the jury
**Deck:** `docs/submission/ComeBack Pitch Deck.pdf` (11 main slides, 6 appendix slides)

The pitch is one story. A mother loses her career. The ICC writes a plan. We put the plan in her pocket. We follow one mother, Sana, step by step. Then we close on why ComeBack should win.

Every slide ends with a question. Say it, pause for one second, then click. The question pulls the judges into the next slide.

## Timing plan

You have 3 minutes plus a short buffer. Clicking, pausing and stage nerves add about 30 seconds, so **practise to 2 minutes 30 seconds without slides**. On stage, that lands near 3 minutes.

| Part | Slides | Time on stage | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story | 1 to 2 | 0:00 to 0:25 | Real players. Make them feel it. |
| Problem | 3 | 0:25 to 0:45 | The ICC has a plan, but it is only a PDF. |
| Solution | 4 to 9 | 0:45 to 2:20 | Walk through the app with Sana. |
| Close | 10 to 11 | 2:20 to 3:00 | Five reasons. End on the ICC's own words. |

The spoken text is about 310 words, down from 475. At your practice speed that is about 2 minutes 15 seconds, which leaves room for clicks and pauses. Do not add words on stage. The slides already show the details, so you do not need to read them out.

## The pitch

### Slide 1. A true story

> She was Sri Lanka's best batter. Hiruka Fernando.
>
> Then she had a baby.
>
> When she came back, her batting fell away. She had to retire.
>
> Was she the only one?

*Tip: wait two seconds before your first word. Let the slide speak first.*

### Slide 2. Was she the only one?

> No. Nain Abidi, Pakistan's first ODI centurion, said: "My heart sank."
>
> For years, a baby meant the end of a career.
>
> So, has anything changed?

### Slide 3. So has anything changed?

> Yes. In June 2026, the ICC wrote a plan for mothers. Six stages, back to cricket.
>
> But it is a PDF. It cannot track her. It cannot check her body. It cannot get a doctor's yes.
>
> What if the plan lived in her pocket?

### Slide 4. Meet ComeBack

> That is ComeBack. She knows her stage. She checks in on camera. Her doctor decides.
>
> Meet Sana. A fast bowler. Her baby is sixteen weeks old.

### Slide 5. Step 1: Where am I?

> Sana opens the app. She sees her stage: Ready. Stage one of six.
>
> Every step shows the ICC page it comes from.
>
> But is her body ready to move?

### Slide 6. Step 2: Is my body ready?

> She puts her phone on the floor and stands on one leg.
>
> The AI counts her reps and times her hold. The video never leaves her phone.
>
> But what if something hurts?

### Slide 7. Step 3: How did it feel?

> The app asks: how did that feel?
>
> One warning sign, and her stage stops. Her body comes first.
>
> So who says yes?

### Slide 8. Step 4: Who decides?

> A real doctor. Never the app.
>
> Her physio sees the results, and presses Approve, or Hold.
>
> And Sana?

### Slide 9. Step 5: What happens next?

> She moves to stage two. In English, Bengali, Hindi or Urdu. Even offline.
>
> So why should ComeBack win?

### Slide 10. Why should ComeBack win?

> One. It is built on the ICC's own plan.
>
> Two. It works today.
>
> Three. A doctor approves every step.
>
> Four. It is for every mother, in any sport.
>
> Five. We are an all-women team, building for women like us.

*Tip: count the five reasons on your fingers.*

### Slide 11. Picture the next mother

> The ICC Chairman said: "No player should have to choose between motherhood and representing her country."
>
> ComeBack makes sure she never has to.
>
> We are Nourin and Ashra. Thank you.

*Tip: slow down on the last two lines. Look at the judges. Smile. Then stop and wait.*

### If you are running long

Cut these first. Together they save about 15 seconds.

- Slide 5: "Every step shows the ICC page it comes from."

## Questions from the jury (2 minutes)

Keep every answer short: one clear sentence, then one example. If a question has a matching appendix slide, jump to it.

| Likely question | Short answer | Slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this a medical device? Who is responsible? | No. ComeBack supports the medical team. It never clears a player. A clinician approves every stage, and any warning symptom stops progress. | Appendix C |
| How accurate is the camera? | We only measure what one phone camera measures well: reps and hold times. Published studies show on-device pose tracking agrees closely with experts on squats. The numbers are notes for the doctor, not a pass or fail. | Appendix F, source 7 |
| What about privacy? | The video never leaves the phone. Only numbers are shared, and only with her consent. The database has no place for video. | Appendix B |
| What does the AI do? | Two things. On the phone, it tracks movement. In the guide, it explains her stage in plain words with citations. It cannot change her stage and it refuses clearance questions. | Appendix C |
| Not many elite players are mothers. Is the market big enough? | It is for every mother who plays: domestic, grassroots and international. And the same six-stage model works for football, netball and more. | Appendix D |
| How will it be paid for? | It is free for players. Boards and federations can run it as part of player welfare, the same way they fund medical staff. | Appendix D |
| Why not use a normal fitness app? | Fitness apps are not linked to a doctor, not based on the ICC plan, and not made for cricket. ComeBack is all three. | Slide 4 |
| What if a player has no doctor? | The app still guides her and stops her if a warning sign appears. But it will not move her forward without a clinician. Boards can give their medical staff access. | Appendix C |
| What would you do next, or with the prize? | First, a clinical review of every rule by sports physios. Then a pilot with one ICC Member board. | Appendix D |
| Can we try it? | Yes. Scan the code on the screen. Demo logins are on the slide. | Appendix A |
| Who is Sana? | A demo player we created to show the journey. All demo data is fictional. | Slide 4 |
| Who built what? | Nourin built the player app, camera AI and design. Ashra built the evidence base, safety rules, secure backend and clinician portal. | Appendix E |

## Delivery tips

- Rehearse with a timer at least five times. Aim for 2 minutes 30 seconds without slides, and under 3 minutes with slides.
- Use the slides, not the live app, during the 3 minutes. The venue Wi-Fi may be slow. Keep the live app open on a phone for the questions.
- Print this script as one page of key lines. Do not read it word for word on stage.
- Speak to the judges. Pause after each question at the end of a slide.
- Before the session, open the PDF in full-screen mode and check that the links on the appendix slides open.
